Constraint-sensitive (10 pairs): Little formal–informal gap overall. The clearest signal is word-count compliance: informal responses violated the target range more often, though there are exceptions (e.g., ID077 where formal missed the constraint).

Constraint-sensitive (ID078): For a highly constrained “2-sentence polite decline,” formal sounded less coherent/more stiff, while informal read smoother. This may be an artifact of the constraints being extremely tight, so it’s a “worth noting, not conclusive” finding.

Dialect-word: Effects are more variable and directionally unstable than constraint-sensitive. Sometimes informal phrasing helps (e.g., ID081 where formal misinterpreted the intent but informal avoided it), but sometimes informal triggers a full semantic miss (e.g., ID080, likely due to weaker context for the dialectal term).

Dialect-word takeaway: When context is not strong, dialectal variants can produce a large negative effect (clear task shift / wrong interpretation). When context is strong, models often recover, sometimes with extra “lexical note” behavior.

Dialect-word verbosity: In a couple cases (e.g., ID082–083), informal responses were slightly more verbose, even when content was similar.

Multislot: Still a near-null result: formal and informal got essentially identical scores, suggesting slot/intent retention in these prompts is not strongly sensitive to formality (at least at this scale).
